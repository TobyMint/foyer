\section{Background and Setup}
\label{sec:bg}

\subsection{Workload}

We replay 200 coding-agent sessions drawn from the TraceLab corpus
\cite{zhu2026tracelab}. Each session is a sequence of rounds; a round presents a
prompt of median 23{,}187 tokens (p90 51{,}064; maximum 89{,}436, which alone is
88\% of the KV pool), and between rounds the session waits on a tool before
returning with a longer context. Arrivals are Poisson at
$\lambda=0.04\,\text{s}^{-1}$ over an 80-minute window, giving 999 steps in
total. Two sessions of maximum size cannot be co-resident: their combined
contexts (89{,}436 and 87{,}821 tokens) exceed the pool by 75\%.

The tool wait is the load's defining property and the one most easily misread.
Its demanded \emph{median} is 0.34\,s, which invites the conclusion that sessions
never leave the engine; its demanded \emph{mean} is 15.82\,s and its maximum is
300\,s, and 18.0\% of the 799 round transitions wait more than ten seconds. Our
replay harness honors the trace: on the run with no controller in the path the
measured gap is 15.82\,s mean against a demanded 15.82\,s, and the median of the
per-transition measured/demanded ratios is 1.001. The controller's own run
measures 16.85\,s, and we attribute the 1.03\,s difference to the controller's
admission delay rather than to the harness.

\subsection{Engine and the binding constraint}

We use SGLang v0.5.10.post1 \cite{zheng2024sglang} with Qwen2.5-Coder-7B-Instruct
\cite{qwen2coder} extended to 96K context, on one RTX 3090 (24\,GB). KV is paged
\cite{kwon2023pagedattention}; at \texttt{mem-fraction-static} 0.85 the pool is
101{,}432 tokens. Hierarchical caching (HiCache), with a host tier of twice the
device pool \cite{flexgen,zerowsap}, is enabled on the configurations marked \emph{+HC} and on every
\sys{} run of this trace; the five plain static caps run without it. We keep both
because the host tier turns out to change the mechanism (\S\ref{sec:model}).
Every run is guarded: a pool that does not come out at $101{,}432 \pm 20$
voids the run rather than recording it.

\subsection{The controller under test}

\sys{} is an admission controller that reads engine telemetry and issues
\emph{named} permits rather than a scalar cap \cite{schedconstraints}: a session is admitted by appearing
in a permit file, and the runner refuses to start a round for a session not
listed. This is what allows per-session actions at all, including the pause
primitive of \S\ref{sec:results:gate}: the controller never frees capacity by
discarding a session, only by admitting fewer, and that is a statement about the
\emph{policy}, not the memory (\S\ref{sec:model}).

Each control cycle (2\,s) the controller computes
\begin{equation}
  \Pi \;=\; \max(\rho, \textstyle\sum_i c_i) \;+\; \textstyle\sum_i g_i ,
  \label{eq:proj}
\end{equation}
where $\rho$ is the engine's reported occupancy, $c_i$ the context length of live
session $i$, and $g_i$ its forecast growth over the next \texttt{horizon} rounds.
A session is admitted when $\Pi + n \le B$, with $n$ the candidate's estimated
need and $B$ the budget: a measured physical occupancy, a reservation for
sessions currently parked in a tool wait (whose KV is evictable and therefore
absent from $\rho$), and a reservation for future growth.

\subsection{Baselines and the verdict criterion}

Baselines are static caps: at most $N$ sessions resident, $N \in \{1,\dots,5\}$,
with and without HiCache. We compare end-to-end wall clock (first submission to
last completion) against the deadline satisfaction rate \cite{liu1973} (SLO
attainment: the fraction of requests whose time-to-first-token is under the
10\,s deadline; the miss rate is one minus it). That clock is the engine's and per request---it
starts when the request is submitted \emph{to the engine}---so a permit wait
outside the door is not charged, the reading most favourable to a controller
that imposes one; \S\ref{sec:discussion} reports the ready clock, on which it is
charged and no run is above the line. We report measured miss rates, not a
latency bound \cite{wilhelm2008}.

``Faster than cap-$N$'' is not the question: the question is whether a policy
reaches a point \emph{above} the static front, because mixing cap-$N$ and
cap-$M$ by time slice approximates any point between two static caps. We flag the segment argument as an argument rather than a curve---carrying
queue state and cache contents across a switch could bend the line---and we have
measured it once: a run that alternates the cap between three and two every 60\,s on
the main trace lands $0.89$\,pp \emph{below} the line at its own wall clock,
inside the tie band and on the below side (\texttt{slice23.csv})---the direction
the argument needs, since that is not where an overstated line would show. It is the stricter of the two criteria, which makes our negative result
\emph{easier} to reach, not harder.

Our best arm reads $-0.30$ and $+0.22$\,pp against the line, and $+5.8$ and
$+6.8$\,pp against the nearest static anchor below it in wall clock
(\texttt{cap3}+HiCache's repeat; $+6.6$ and $+7.6$ against that anchor's
\emph{first} run---so the sign of the comparison is not in question, but its
size moves with which run and which repeat you take). The arm whose repeats
bracket the line most widely is a different one---\texttt{horizon}=1, landing
at $0.93$, $5.58$ and $3.22$\,pp below it across its three runs---and we name
the run we mean whenever we quote a distance for it, because the spread across
those runs ($4.4$\,pp of SLO) is wider than the margin that decides our
headline.

We take the upper-left envelope of the static points and report each
controller's signed distance above or below it: more than $+1.0$\,pp is above
the front, less than $-1.0$\,pp below it, and the band between the two is
\emph{on} it---a tie, not a win: no distinguishable improvement observed. The
band is a convention we fixed before
running anything, not a calibrated confidence bound. Our later replicate measurements bracket it rather than
justify it: the SLO spread of configurations we ran more than once ranges from
0.5\,pp to 5.1\,pp, so for some configurations it is generous, for others far too
narrow. We flag this as a weakness of the criterion, not a
defence of it.

One property of the envelope deserves flagging: one row in
Table~\ref{tab:runs}'s controller section---the all-throttles-off arm, which
runs a different controller---finishes slower than 295 minutes, judged against the
right-hand segment from \texttt{cap2}+HiCache at 295 minutes to
\texttt{cap1} at 527, and \texttt{cap1} is the only anchor there that runs
\emph{without} HiCache, so it is an interpolation between one host-tier point
and one device-only point. We report the conservative
reading: \emph{below the front as we defined it}, not \emph{below every static
configuration}.
