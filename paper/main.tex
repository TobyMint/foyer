\begin{abstract}
An agentic LLM session is a long, multi-round job whose KV footprint grows every
round, each carrying a first-token deadline; on a memory-constrained
accelerator the pool binds, and admission control---which rounds to admit---looks
like the lever. Busy-time service rate is $\overline{R}/\tau$, so raising
concurrency by $f$ buys $f^{1-\epsilon}$, $\epsilon$ the elasticity of per-step
\emph{service time} to the running count---the step, not the request, whose
completion time carries queueing and step count. On a memory-bound
agentic replay $\epsilon = 0.38$ to $0.61$, and it tracks the memory tier, not the
controller---an association across five policies, not a
demonstration. With a host tier four static points hold 87.9--92.4\% of the reuse
bound over $\overline{R}_{\text{busy}}$ 1.25 to 1.75; without one, realization
falls from 92.5\% to 36.4\%, as recomputation. The verdict is negative: nine
controller variants spanning accounting, growth reserve and execution
granularity, two more on the budget ceiling, and an independent congestion-based
controller reimplemented---none above the static admission baseline for the same
trace, deadline and resource budget, the best twice-measured at $-0.30$ and
$+0.22$\,pp of deadline satisfaction rate, both on the line: no
improvement beyond the $\pm1.0$\,pp band fixed in advance. A tenth arm---the same
controller revision, one expression
changed---lands above it twice in three runs, by $1.3$ and $2.2$\,pp, the third on
the line: both readings are the \emph{release} rule's, not the admission decision's. A second GPU and a second trace reproduce the direction, not the level. Our criterion is a
diagnostic: it neither returns the verdict nor explains the failure. Miss rates
are measured, not a latency bound.
\end{abstract}
