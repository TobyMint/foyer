\section{The Same Measurements on a Second Workload}
\label{sec:xtrace}

Everything so far is one trace family, and a negative result drawn from one
family invites the objection that the family is the finding. We ran the same
accounting, a static sweep from a cap of two to a cap of eight, and the same
controller on a second replay:
117 real Claude agent sessions, 390 rounds, the same model, the same engine and
the same 101{,}432-token pool. One difference has to be stated up front,
because it bears on the paper's central variable: \emph{this trace was run
entirely without a host tier}. Its static points, its envelope and its
controller are all device-only. That makes the comparison internally fair---both
sides of the front are the same configuration---but it means this workload
tests the negative result and not the tier mechanism, and it leaves an
alternative account of the controller's failure standing: it failed here on the
configuration where \S\ref{sec:model} says added concurrency is expensive. The two workloads differ in ways that matter for
this paper, which is why we chose it. Its sessions are smaller (median prompt
18{,}809 tokens against 23{,}187) and its tool waits are far longer at the
median: over round transitions---same session,
adjacent rounds, each session's last round excluded because it has no
successor---the gap is 20.2\,s against 0.34\,s at the median---about 60$\times$---and
39.9\,s against 15.8\,s at the mean, which is 2.5$\times$
(\texttt{toolwait.csv} carries both traces on this definition). The 29.1\,s
that appears in \texttt{elasticity\_x.csv} is the mean over all 390 rows
including each session's last round, and is not comparable with the main
trace's 15.8\,s; we use the transition definition throughout §\ref{sec:xtrace}. So the engine is idle far more of the
time, and the running counts are lower.

\subsection{What transfers, and what does not}

\begin{figure}[tb]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{figs/fig8_transfer.pdf}
  \caption{Realization against the running count on both traces, each
  normalized to its own trace's reuse bound. Device-only, the main trace falls
  from 92.5\% to 36.4\% and the second from 74.8\% to 57.5\%; with a host
  tier the main trace's runs stay at 72.8--92.5\%. The second trace is lower
  throughout---interpolating the main trace's line at the second trace's
  running counts gives a 17-point gap at the lowest and 23 at the
  highest---and it declines more gently, which is what ``the direction
  transfers and the level does not'' looks like on this axis.}
  \label{fig:transfer}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{The reuse accounting transfers in direction, not in level.} The
second trace plans a higher reuse bound than the first---86.3\% of its prompt
tokens against 75.5\%---and realizes less of it, at a lower device occupancy
(Fig.~\ref{fig:transfer}).
Realization falls from 74.8\% to 57.5\% as the running count rises from 1.08 to
1.50, so the direction of \S\ref{sec:model} survives. What does \emph{not}
survive is the sharpness. On the main trace the decline is a threshold: the
per-unit slope is $-10.4$, $-40.6$, then $-157.1$ and $-127.6$ points per unit
as the running count passes 1.53, which is the cliff \S\ref{sec:criterion}
measures. Here the slope stays gentler through the same region---$-31.2$,
$-36.8$, $-28.4$, $-93.0$ and $-67.6$---and the two steep numbers at the end
sit on spans of 0.017 and 0.074 of running count, where a difference between
two runs starts to read as a trend. Taken as a whole the sweep loses 74.8--57.5\%
of this trace's bound between $\overline{R}$ 1.08 and 1.50 at $-40.9$ points
per unit. We do compare the two
traces' overall slopes, which is the one slope comparison the data supports
because both are taken over their full measured ranges
(\texttt{slope.csv}): the device-only family runs from 92.5\% to 36.4\% over
$\overline{R}$ 1.000--1.842, a whole-range slope of $-66.8$ points per unit,
and this trace from 74.8\% to 57.5\% over 1.08--1.50, a slope of $-40.9$.
So per unit of running count, the main trace loses its bound about one and a
half times as fast over its range ($-66.8$ against $-40.9$)---but the
comparison is between ranges of different length and
different absolute level, and we do not lean on it.
What this trace does establish is that the \emph{level} does not transfer, and
we treat that as a bound on what we can
claim: it realizes \emph{less} at 15.8\% median device occupancy than the
main trace does at 22.4\%---the two numbers are the lightest cap on each trace
(\texttt{realhr\_cap2} realizing 74.8\% and \texttt{cap1} realizing 92.5\%), both
medians of \texttt{sglang:token\_usage} with means of 13.9\% and 25.2\%
(\texttt{reuse\_x.csv}, \texttt{occupancy.csv})---so realization is not a
function of occupancy alone and a
rule of the form ``the pool collapses above $x$\% occupancy'' is false on our
own data. Occupancy is instantaneous; reuse depends on whether a prefix
survives from one round to the next, which is a history the instantaneous
number does not carry. We do not measure that history here, and we say so
rather than fitting the two traces to a common curve.

\paragraph{The elasticity criterion transfers in sign, not in place.} On both
traces the criterion plays the same role, and it is the modest one
\S\ref{sec:criterion} settles on: it \emph{rules out} the workload-side
story. The controller on this trace runs at a busy-time mean of 1.223, which
falls inside the span its own static caps cover (1.08 to 1.50)---more
precisely inside the cap3$\to$cap4 segment, where
$\varepsilon(\tau_1) = -0.28$ over $\overline{R}$ 1.16--1.29, so its failure is
again not explained by the criterion.
What the completed sweep adds is that this trace does approach the criterion's
\emph{expensive} branch, later and more gently than the main trace: above
$\overline{R} \approx 1.29$ the model-consistent elasticity turns positive and
touches one in the first of the three segments there ($+1.0$, then $+0.18$ and
$+0.87$ as the sweep reaches 1.50), while the main trace crosses one at 1.53
with $+1.15$ and stays above it. So the direction is not a property of one
family; the location is. We report the point estimates and not a threshold, because a $\pm10\%$
perturbation of $\tau_1$---inside the range our repeat pairs show---carries
each of those three segments' bounds across zero \emph{and} across one. The per-request
measure is no steadier ($+0.45$, $-1.25$, $+0.73$). And the controller never
operates where the branch turns expensive: 1.22, below 1.29 and below the main
trace's 1.53 alike.

We also record what the noise does, because it is the same weakness the whole
paper has. Recomputing each segment's $\varepsilon$ with $\tau_1$ perturbed
by $\pm10$\%---against the 0.3--1.1\% our clean repeat pairs differ by in
$\tau_1$ (\texttt{decomp.csv}; one anomalous pair reaches 42.5\%), so the
perturbation is generous rather than conservative---gives bands of
$[-5.20, +0.41]$ for cap2$\to$cap3,
$[-2.17, +1.61]$ for cap3$\to$cap4, $[-1.25, +3.26]$ for cap4$\to$cap5,
$[-14.07, +14.43]$ for cap5$\to$cap6 and $[-3.33, +5.07]$ for
cap6$\to$cap8. Every one of them crosses zero, and the three at the end cross
one as well. So at that perturbation no segment's sign is resolvable, and the
turn the paragraph above describes is a property of the point estimates rather
than a boundary we have located---which is a bound on that paragraph, not a
reprieve from it.

\paragraph{The controller's verdict transfers, weakly.} \sys{} finishes the
second trace in 75.6 minutes, against 66.2 for the fastest static run on it
(the cap of eight), so \sys{} is 14.3\% slower (14.26\% at full precision).
We do not report that as a loss. The repeat pairs we have vary by 1.8--4.7\%
in wall clock (\texttt{replicates.csv}), and this trace gives us two of its
own on the same clock (\texttt{repeat\_spread.csv}): the cap of four repeats
to within 1.6\%, and \sys{} itself to within 0.02\%, the latter accompanied
by 0.03 points of realization on the volume axis. The gap is more than
eight times the wider of those, so it is not repeat noise on either side of
it---but two pairs cannot bound the variability of the \emph{other}
configurations in the comparison, and run-to-run spread on this machine
reaches 22.0\% across the
other configurations and tiers we have measured (\texttt{repeat\_spread.csv};
0.02--6.08\% across the ten pairs those two traces give us, the spread between
the pairs themselves being the reason we report a range and not a number).
So the honest reading is the one we have used throughout: at the threshold
this paper uses, it did not win on
either axis.
Its signed distance from the second trace's
static envelope is $+0.40$\,pp at $10$\,s on
the SLO axis---inside the $\pm1.0$\,pp tie band, which is defined on that
axis---and $-0.14$\,pp on the cache-hit axis, against the 14.9 points that
separate this trace's own six static caps on that axis. That axis is
threshold-dependent here, and in the opposite direction from the main trace's:
at $5$\,s this trace's caps separate much more (the cap of eight scores
$78.2$\% against the cap of two's $99.0$\%) and the controller sits
$+1.87$\,pp above the envelope---outside the band, a win on that axis---whereas
on the main trace no controller reaches the front at $5$\,s and one crosses at
$20$\,s (\S\ref{sec:discussion}). We report both readings rather than the one
that gives a single answer. (The envelope and the
elasticity below are both built from all six static caps the sweep now has;
the three caps the sweep added after the first version of this comparison---the
caps of five, six and eight---are the reason the last three segments exist
at all.)
The SLO number is nearly uninformative, and we flag that rather than let it
stand as a measurement. At the three caps the envelope was originally built
from, every point on this trace scored 99.5--100.0\%, so the axis could not
discriminate at all. The three the sweep has since added do separate---the cap
of eight scores 93.8\% against the cap of two's 100.0\%---so the axis is not
flat after all, but its whole spread over six static caps is 6.2 points against
a $\pm1.0$\,pp tie band, and \sys{} sits at 100.0\% on it.

\subsection{What this section does and does not buy}

It buys a negative result that is not a property of one trace family: on a
second workload, with a different session-size distribution and a
tool-wait distribution about 60$\times$ longer at the median, the same controller
still does not beat the same kind of static cap, and the reuse degradation
still appears. It costs us the transportability of the threshold. On the main trace the
criterion narrowed the search by excluding the workload-side explanation and
put the turn of the point estimates at $\overline{R} \approx 1.53$; on this
trace the same measurement turns positive above 1.29. What we cannot do is
\emph{locate} either turn---the $\pm10\%$ perturbation puts every segment's
bounds across zero on both traces---which is the caveat
\S\ref{sec:criterion} already states for the main trace, now with a second
trace behind it. The controller is below both turns (1.22), so the criterion
does not explain its failure on either trace. We
report both, and we note that the second workload is closer to the regime
where adaptive control \emph{should} pay---lower concurrency, much longer idle
gaps---and the controller still did not win there.
