\section{The Same Measurements on a Second Workload}
\label{sec:xtrace}

Everything so far is one trace family, and a negative result drawn from one
family invites the objection that the family is the finding. We ran the same
accounting, the same static sweep and the same controller on a second replay:
117 real Claude agent sessions, 390 rounds, the same model, the same engine and
the same 101{,}432-token pool. One difference has to be stated up front,
because it bears on the paper's central variable: \emph{this trace was run
entirely without a host tier}. Its static points, its envelope and its
controller are all device-only. That makes the comparison internally fair---both
sides of the front are the same configuration---but it means this workload
tests the negative result and not the tier mechanism, and it leaves an
alternative account of the controller's failure standing: it failed here on the
configuration where \S\ref{sec:model} says added concurrency is expensive. The two workloads differ in ways that matter for
this paper, which is why we chose it. Its sessions are smaller (median prompt
18{,}809 tokens against 23{,}187) and its tool waits are two orders of
magnitude longer at the median: over round transitions---same session,
adjacent rounds, each session's last round excluded because it has no
successor---the demanded gap is 20.2\,s against 0.34\,s on the main trace, a
59.8$\times$ difference, and the mean is 29.1\,s against 15.8\,s
(\texttt{toolwait.csv}; \texttt{elasticity\_x.csv}). So the engine is idle far more of the
time, and the running counts are lower.

\subsection{What transfers, and what does not}

\paragraph{The reuse accounting transfers in direction, not in level.} The
second trace plans a higher reuse bound than the first---86.3\% of its prompt
tokens against 75.5\%---and realizes less of it, at a lower device occupancy.
Realization falls from 74.8\% to 64.0\% as the running count rises from 1.08 to
1.41, so the direction of \S\ref{sec:model} survives. What does \emph{not}
survive is the shape. On the main trace the decline is a threshold: the
per-unit slope is $-10.8$, $-41.0$, then $-157.4$ and $-126.7$ points per unit
as the running count passes 1.53, which is the cliff \S\ref{sec:criterion}
measures. Here the slope is roughly constant---$-31.3$, $-36.6$, $-28.4$---over
a span that reaches 1.41, so at 1.41 this trace has lost more of its bound
than the main trace had at 1.53 (36\% against 21\%) and has not yet begun to
fall off a cliff. The cliff is a threshold that this trace has not reached at
the running counts its own offered load produces; whether it has one at all,
and where, is what the rest of the sweep is for. We do not compare the
two traces' slopes: ours would be computed over different spans of the
running-count axis, and the device-only family's overall slope
($-66.8$ points per unit over its whole range) is steeper than this trace's
three-point span ($-34.5$) anyway, so the comparison would say the opposite of
what the raw direction does. The level does not, and we treat that as a bound on what we can
claim: this trace realizes \emph{less} at 15.8\% median device occupancy than the
main trace does at 22.4\% (both medians of \texttt{sglang:token\_usage}; the
means are 13.9\% and 25.2\%), so realization is not a function of occupancy alone and a
rule of the form ``the pool collapses above $x$\% occupancy'' is false on our
own data. Occupancy is instantaneous; reuse depends on whether a prefix
survives from one round to the next, which is a history the instantaneous
number does not carry. We do not measure that history here, and we say so
rather than fitting the two traces to a common curve.

\paragraph{The elasticity criterion does not transfer, and this is the
sharper result.} On the main trace the criterion's role is to \emph{rule out} the
workload-side story---it shows the controllers were not in a segment where
added concurrency was self-defeating, which is why \S\ref{sec:criterion}
relocates the failure to the controller. On the second trace even that weaker
role fails, because the controller never goes near the segment the criterion
is about. Its running count during busy time is
1.223, below every static point we ran on that trace (1.077, 1.161, 1.287), and
the segment it lands in has $\varepsilon(\tau_1) = -0.28$ over
$\overline{R}$ 1.16--1.29---concurrency is close to free there. The measured
segments on this trace are $-2.40$ (cap2$\to$cap3) and $-0.28$
(cap3$\to$cap4), both negative, and the highest
running count we measure on this trace---a mean of 1.287 over busy time, with
an instantaneous peak of four in flight---stays below the
$\overline{R} \approx 1.5$ where the main trace's cliff begins. So
the criterion's expensive segment is \emph{untested} here---neither confirmed
nor refuted---and the one thing this trace does establish is that the criterion
cannot be doing any explanatory work for this controller either, since the
controller failed without ever entering the region the criterion is about.

We also record what the noise does to this measurement, because it is the same
weakness the whole paper has. Recomputing each segment's $\varepsilon$ with
$\tau_1$ perturbed by $\pm10$\%, a perturbation inside the range our repeat
pairs actually show (1.2--22.4\%), gives $[-5.20, +0.41]$ for cap2$\to$cap3 and $[-2.17, +1.61]$ for
cap3$\to$cap4. Both bands cross one, so on this trace the criterion's sign is
not resolvable either.

\paragraph{The controller's verdict transfers, weakly.} \sys{} finishes the
second trace in 75.6 minutes against 66.9 for the fastest static cap, so it is
13.2\% slower (13.15\% at full precision)---but that gap is inside the run-to-run spread we measure
elsewhere (up to 22.4\% between repeat pairs of the same configuration), so we
do not claim it lost. What we can say is that it did not win, on either axis:
its signed distance from the second trace's static envelope is $+0.40$\,pp on
the SLO axis and $-0.14$\,pp on the cache-hit axis, both inside the tie band.
The SLO number is nearly uninformative, and we flag that rather than let it
stand as a measurement: at a 10\,s threshold every point on this trace scores
99.5--100.0\%, so the axis cannot discriminate and ``on the line'' means only
that nobody violated 10\,s.

\subsection{What this section does and does not buy}

It buys a negative result that is not a property of one trace family: on a
second workload, with a different session-size distribution and a
tool-wait distribution 60$\times$ longer at the median, the same controller
still does not beat the same kind of static cap, and the reuse degradation
still appears. It costs us the transportability of the mechanism. On the main trace the criterion narrowed the search by excluding the
workload-side explanation; on this one it cannot even do that, because the
controller never reaches the region the criterion describes. We
report both, and we note that the second workload is closer to the regime
where adaptive control \emph{should} pay---lower concurrency, much longer idle
gaps---and the controller still did not win there.
