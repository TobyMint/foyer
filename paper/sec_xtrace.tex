\section{The Same Measurements on a Second Workload}
\label{sec:xtrace}

Everything so far is one trace family, and a negative result drawn from one
family invites the objection that the family is the finding. We ran the same
accounting, the same static sweep and the same controller on a second replay:
117 real Claude agent sessions, 390 rounds, the same model, the same engine and
the same 101{,}432-token pool. The two workloads differ in ways that matter for
this paper, which is why we chose it. Its sessions are smaller (median prompt
18{,}809 tokens against 23{,}187) and its tool waits are an order of magnitude
longer: the median inter-round gap is 10.6\,s against 73\,ms on the main trace,
and the mean is 29.1\,s against 12.7\,s. So the engine is idle far more of the
time, and the running counts are lower.

\subsection{What transfers, and what does not}

\paragraph{The reuse accounting transfers in direction, not in level.} The
second trace plans a higher reuse bound than the first---86.3\% of its prompt
tokens against 75.5\%---and realizes less of it, at a lower device occupancy.
Realization falls from 74.8\% to 67.5\% as the running count rises from 1.08 to
1.29, a steeper per-unit drop than the device-only family's ($-34.5$ against
$-11.1$ points per unit $\overline{R}$), so the direction of \S\ref{sec:model}
survives. The level does not, and we treat that as a bound on what we can
claim: this trace realizes \emph{less} at 15.8\% mean occupancy than the main
trace does at 22.4\%, so realization is not a function of occupancy alone and a
rule of the form ``the pool collapses above $x$\% occupancy'' is false on our
own data. Occupancy is instantaneous; reuse depends on whether a prefix
survives from one round to the next, which is a history the instantaneous
number does not carry. We do not measure that history here, and we say so
rather than fitting the two traces to a common curve.

\paragraph{The elasticity criterion does not transfer, and this is the
sharper result.} On the main trace the criterion's explanation of our
controllers' failure is that they sit where $\varepsilon \ge 1$, so added
concurrency does not pay. On the second trace that explanation cannot apply,
because the controller never goes there. Its running count during busy time is
1.223, below every static point we ran on that trace (1.077, 1.161, 1.287), and
the segment it lands in has $\varepsilon(\tau_1) = -0.28$ over
$\overline{R}$ 1.16--1.29---concurrency is close to free there. The measured
segments on this trace are $-2.40$ (cap2$\to$cap3) and $-0.28$
(cap3$\to$cap4), both negative, and the trace's maximum running count never
reaches the $\overline{R} \approx 1.5$ where the main trace's cliff begins. So
the criterion's expensive segment is \emph{untested} on the second trace, not
confirmed and not refuted; what is refuted is the mechanism \S\ref{sec:criterion}
attributes to our controllers, because here the controller failed without ever
entering it.

We also record what the noise does to this measurement, because it is the same
weakness the whole paper has. Recomputing each segment's $\varepsilon$ with
$\tau_1$ perturbed by $\pm10$\%---the spread our repeat pairs actually show
---gives $[-5.20, +0.41]$ for cap2$\to$cap3 and $[-2.17, +1.61]$ for
cap3$\to$cap4. Both bands cross one, so on this trace the criterion's sign is
not resolvable either.

\paragraph{The controller's verdict transfers, weakly.} \sys{} finishes the
second trace in 75.6 minutes against 66.9 for the fastest static cap, so it is
13.2\% slower---but that gap is inside the run-to-run spread we measure
elsewhere (up to 22.4\% between repeat pairs of the same configuration), so we
do not claim it lost. What we can say is that it did not win, on either axis:
its signed distance from the second trace's static envelope is $+0.40$\,pp on
the SLO axis and $-0.14$\,pp on the cache-hit axis, both inside the tie band.
The SLO number is nearly uninformative, and we flag that rather than let it
stand as a measurement: at a 10\,s threshold every point on this trace scores
99.5--100.0\%, so the axis cannot discriminate and ``on the line'' means only
that nobody violated 10\,s.

\subsection{What this section does and does not buy}

It buys a negative result that is not a property of one trace family: on a
second workload, with a different session-size distribution and a
tool-wait distribution 145$\times$ longer at the median, the same controller
still does not beat the same kind of static cap, and the reuse degradation
still appears. It costs us the transportability of the mechanism. The
criterion of \S\ref{sec:criterion} was built to say \emph{why} adaptive
admission fails, and on the second workload it does not say that, because the
controller is not operating where the criterion says failure comes from. We
report both, and we note that the second workload is closer to the regime
where adaptive control \emph{should} pay---lower concurrency, much longer idle
gaps---and the controller still did not win there.
