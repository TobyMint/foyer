    55	\subsection{The controller under test}
    56	
    57	\sys{} is an admission controller that reads engine telemetry and issues
    58	\emph{named} permits rather than a scalar cap: a session is admitted by
    59	appearing in a permit file, and the runner refuses to start a round for a
    60	session that is not listed. This design choice matters for the comparison,
    61	because it is what allows per-session actions at all---including the pause
    62	primitive we exploit in \S\ref{sec:results:gate}. It also means that a session,
    63	once admitted, holds its permit for its entire lifetime, so the controller
    64	never frees capacity by discarding a session's state---it admits fewer sessions
    65	instead. That is a statement about the \emph{policy}, not about the memory: the
    66	engine's radix cache still evicts parked prefixes under pressure, and we
    67	measure that it does and that they are recomputed (\S\ref{sec:model}). We
    68	return to this distinction in \S\ref{sec:discussion}, because it bounds how far
    69	our negative result generalises.
    70	
    71	Each control cycle (2\,s) the controller computes
    72	\begin{equation}
    73	  \Pi \;=\; \max(\rho, \textstyle\sum_i c_i) \;+\; \textstyle\sum_i g_i ,
    74	  \label{eq:proj}
    75	\end{equation}
    76	where $\rho$ is the engine's reported occupancy, $c_i$ the context length of
    77	live session $i$, and $g_i$ its forecast growth over the next
    78	\texttt{horizon} rounds. A session is admitted when $\Pi + n \le B$, with $n$
    79	the candidate's estimated need and $B$ the budget. The three terms are
    80	respectively the measured physical occupancy, a reservation for sessions
    81	currently parked in a tool wait (whose KV is evictable and therefore absent
    82	from $\rho$), and a reservation for future growth.

    84	\subsection{Baselines and the verdict criterion}
    85	
    86	Baselines are static caps: at most $N$ sessions resident, $N \in \{1,\dots,5\}$,
    87	with and without HiCache. We compare on two axes: end-to-end wall clock
    88	(time from the first submission to the last completion) and SLO attainment
    89	(fraction of requests whose time-to-first-token is under 10\,s).
    90	
    91	The verdict criterion deserves care, because ``faster than cap-$N$'' is not
    92	the question. The question is whether a policy reaches a point \emph{above}
    93	the static front. A point that lies merely between two static caps is not a
    94	win: mixing cap-$N$ and cap-$M$ by time slice approximates any point on the
    95	line between them. We flag this segment argument as an argument, not a
    96	measurement---we did not run the time-sliced policies, and carrying queue state
    97	and cache contents across a switch could bend the line. It is the criterion we
    98	apply, and it is the stricter of the two we could have used---stricter than
    99	comparing each controller against the discrete static points alone, because the
   100	line sits above them. We state the direction plainly rather than claim it as a
   101	caution: this choice makes our negative result \emph{easier} to reach, not
   102	harder. Our best arm is $0.55$\,pp below the line and would be $+4.4$\,pp
   103	against the nearest static anchor below it in wall clock (\texttt{cap3}+HiCache's
   104	repeat, 81.9\% at 230.3 minutes; $+5.2$ would be the comparison against that
   105	anchor's first run at 81.1\% and 236.4 minutes, which is the one it displaced).
   106	The arm we repeated most tightly is a different one---\texttt{horizon}=1, at
   107	$0.93$\,pp below the line and $+3.7$\,pp against the same first run---and we
   108	name both because ``best'' depends on which of the two the reader means. So we take the
   109	upper-left
   110	envelope of the static points and report each controller's signed distance
   111	above or below it. We classify a signed distance of more than $+1.0$\,pp as
   112	above the front, less than $-1.0$\,pp as below it, and the band between the
   113	two as \emph{on} it---a tie, not a win. The band is a convention we fixed
   114	before running anything, and we report it here because every verdict in this
   115	paper depends on it. Our later replicate measurements (\S\ref{sec:results})
